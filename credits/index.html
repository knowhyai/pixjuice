Credits & licenses

PixJuice is a thin studio around other people's excellent pixel art. Here is who made what, and the licenses that let us ship it.

The effects: pixelfx

Effects from pixelfx by Patch and Hugo Duprez, MIT License. All 244 effects in this catalog — fire, explosions, ice, melee, arcane, weather and the rest — are rendered live from the pixelfx library's own code. Nothing here would exist without it.

pixelfx itself builds on more good work, described in its NOTICE file: src/core/sim.js and some effects adapt Hugo Duprez's open-source pixel art VFX (MIT), and src/core/act.js adapts ideas from Hollow Crown (MIT).

The sounds: jsfxr

The 8-bit sound effects are generated in your browser with jsfxr — a JavaScript port of sfxr by DrPetter, by Eric Fredricksen, maintained by Chris McCormick — released under The Unlicense. Each effect family carries a hand-tuned sfxr preset, and every WAV you download is rendered from it; no audio files are fetched.

  • jsfxr UNLICENSE — full text (public domain)
  • sfxr.me — the online jsfxr generator

What's PixJuice's, and what isn't

  • PixJuice is not "pixelfx". pixelfx is the upstream library and its name belongs to its authors. PixJuice is an independent tool built on top of it, and is not affiliated with or endorsed by Patch or Hugo Duprez.
  • Fonts: this site uses your system UI fonts. The pixel font used by the official pixelfx gallery ("Patch Pixel") is not MIT-licensed and is deliberately not used here.
  • GIF encoding is done in the browser with gifenc (MIT).
  • MP3 encoding is done in the browser with lamejs (LGPL-3.0), loaded only when you export MP3s. OGG uses the browser's own Opus encoder.
  • The PixJuice app code (editor, exports, sound synthesis, styling) is MIT too; the exported effect art is covered by pixelfx's MIT license, so you can use it in your games, commercial included.

Exported files & attribution

Every exported sprite sheet and JSON sidecar records where the art came from: the JSON's meta.app points at the pixelfx repository and meta.pixelfx.id names the effect. You don't owe PixJuice anything for exports — but a nod to pixelfx in your credits screen would make the artists' day.
