How to convert an image to pixel art

  1. Drop any photo or PNG onto the page (Ctrl/⌘+V pastes from the clipboard too). Nothing is uploaded to any server.
  2. Dial it in: pixel size sets how blocky the result is; pick an automatic reduced palette, a built-in classic, or load a Lospec palette / paste hex codes; add dithering for gradients, remove a solid background, and trace a 1-block outline.
  3. Export a transparent PNG at 1× for engines and editors, or at 2×–8× for social posts and store pages — then upscale it further, slice it into a sprite sheet, or light it with a normal map.

Image to pixel art FAQ

How do I convert an image to pixel art?

Drop a photo or PNG onto the page, choose a pixel size (how many source pixels become one block), pick a palette or let PixJuice reduce the colors automatically, then download the PNG. Everything runs in your browser, so the image is never uploaded.

What does dithering do in a pixel art converter?

Dithering trades colors for texture: with fewer colors than the original, ordered (Bayer) or Floyd–Steinberg dithering mixes palette colors in a dot pattern, which preserves shading gradients instead of producing flat bands.

Can I use a Lospec palette?

Yes. Type a Lospec palette name (for example endesga-32) and press load, or paste a list of hex colors like #ff0044 #ffd9e0. PixJuice maps every pixel to the closest color of that palette.

How do I remove the background when converting a photo to pixel art?

Set background to corner color for a solid matte or sky, or pick a custom key color, then raise the tolerance until the background turns transparent. The exported PNG keeps the transparency.

Yes — single-image conversion with every option (palettes, dithering, background removal, outline, PNG export at 1× or scaled up) is completely free, with no account and no upload.
