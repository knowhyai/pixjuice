Five layers, stack them yourself

1 · Delayed white bar

Damage does not leave the bar instantly: the lost chunk stays white for a moment, then catches up. The player reads how big the hit was from the gap.

The wait-then-catch rhythm is long-standing boss-bar practice; the 0.35 s wait / 0.45 s catch values are tunable defaults, not measured standards.

Pitfall A ghost that never catches up lies to the player. Restart the hold on rapid hits if you like, but the chase must always finish.

2 · Hit shake & flash

The bar itself reacts: a short decaying shake and a brief white flash on every hit, a touch harder when a layer breaks.

Shake that decays within a fraction of a second follows the screenshake practice on the hit-feel page (Nijman 2013; Song 2005); 0.27 s / 14 px / 0.08 s here are tunable defaults.

Pitfall Shaking the whole HUD makes the interface feel broken. Shake the bar, not the screen — and skip the shake entirely under reduced motion.

3 · Scale ticks

Fine lines every 10% give the eye a ruler for how far the boss has fallen; small triangle markers at 25 / 50 / 75 flag the phases ahead.

Quarter marks usually match phase changes rather than any published rule — the 10% step and the quarter markers are tunable defaults.

Pitfall Ticks every 5% turn the bar into a ruler you read instead of a bar you feel. Keep them quiet.

4 · Layered HP

The boss carries several health layers: the bar drains purple, then orange, then red, and a ×N counter shows how many layers are left. Breaking a layer is its own event.

Color order and the ×N badge are genre convention (fighting-game boss bars), not a measured finding — tunable defaults. Keep the count small; three reads, seven doesn't.

Pitfall If the layer break has no reaction of its own (flash, sound), players read it as a bug — the bar jumped, not the fight.

5 · Low-health alarm

Under the threshold the bar pulses red with a glow, and — if you want it — the edge of the screen warns red. In this demo the edge stays inside the demo stage, never the whole page.

The 25% threshold and 2.5 Hz pulse are tunable defaults. A pulse fast enough to feel urgent is also fast enough to annoy — offer reduced motion.

Pitfall A full-screen red vignette that ignores the player's reduced-motion setting is a motion-sickness trap. Respect prefers-reduced-motion from day one.

✦ Reduced motion

Not a juice layer: an accessibility switch for the whole demo. Auto follows the OS preference; On replaces every pulse and shake with a steady state that carries the same information.

Hookup & limits
  • Targets Godot 4.x (a Control script — the header comment lists the node tree to build: Track, LagFill, Fill, Ticks, labels, optional edge vignette) and any browser for the web tab (one self-contained HTML file).
  • Hookup: feed damage through take_damage(units) / heal(units); setup(fraction) starts a fight. The low-HP threshold compares against the boss’s total HP across all layers, not the active layer.
  • Every number is a tunable default exported from the lab — retune it for your project; it is a starting point, not a rule.
  • The screen-edge warning belongs in your HUD layer, never on the window root; the exported snippets honor prefers-reduced-motion / the reduce_motion flag on their own.

Sources & method

Slider numbers are tunable defaults — starting points to retune for your project, not universal rules. Published sources are cited separately below; unlabeled numbers come from action-game practice (the industry's oral tradition), not research. Retune for your game.
