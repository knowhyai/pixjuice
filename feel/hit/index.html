Hit feel — six layers you can stack

Two rigged knights in an arena: strike the head, chest or belly and tune what the hit feels like — hit stop, screen shake, flash/squash/numbers, knockback, directional sparks and synthesized three-band audio. Every switch and slider rewrites the Godot 4 and Unity code below.

Six layers, stack them yourself

1 · Hit stop

Freeze the whole simulation for a few frames at the moment of impact. The pause is the weight: everything stops, then releases together.

Published anchors: action games freeze “a few frames” (Song 2005); Guilty Gear Xrd ≈7 light / ≈10 heavy (Lin et al. 2022); Smash Ultimate ≈15 frames at 15% damage, 30-frame cap (SmashWiki). The 3–4 / 10+ starting points below are heuristics.

Pitfall Past ~10 frames it starts reading as a stutter rather than weight — the player thinks the game dropped frames.

2 · Screen shake

Add trauma on impact and shake the camera by trauma², biased along the attack direction, with a fast, smooth decay.

Directional, short and fast with quick decay — practice from Nijman (2013) and Song (2005); Lin et al. (2022) count camera control among the three make-or-break factors. Amplitudes have no published standard: heuristic.

Pitfall Fresh random jitter every frame reads as a glitch. During hit stop, decay trauma with unscaled time — or the shake freezes with the world.

3 · Hit feedback

One layer, three parts: a white flash, a local squash of the body part you struck, and one readable damage number. Each part has its own switch.

A 1–2 frame flash matches the range Jonasson & Purho demo (2012); exact counts are heuristics. Damage numbers come from Chinese studio practice (2014), not from a paper.

Pitfall Flash + squash + a wall of numbers on one hit is noise, not feedback — the overdone preset shows exactly how bad it gets.

4 · Knockback

Launch the victim along the hit direction with constant deceleration and a hard stop — the stop is where the mass reads.

Direction follows the attack line (Song 2005; Nijman 2013). No published distance/duration table — the slider values are heuristics; compare with the presets.

Pitfall A uniform slide feels weightless. Fast out, sudden stop — never a constant-velocity glide.

5 · Directional VFX

Sparks and a shockwave that travel with the force, into and through the target — the burst inherits the attack direction.

Particles and hit light appear in the Juice It or Lose It demo and Song (2005); counts have no standard — start restrained (heuristic), pile on only in the overdone preset, per Kao (2020).

Pitfall An isotropic burst at the impact point reads as confetti. Aim the cone along the hit direction.

6 · Three-band audio

Synthesized live in the page: the low band carries weight, the mid band texture, the high band crispness. Each band has its own switch.

The layer structure (whoosh / effort / impact / reaction) is from Song (2005); “low = weight, mid = texture, high = crisp” is a common heuristic, not a published model. Lin et al. (2022) list sound consistency as a make-or-break factor.

Pitfall Stacking all three bands on every single hit washes the sound out — mix per attack weight instead.

Sources & method

Numbers on this page carry one of two labels: a published source, or 经验值 (heuristic) — the industry's oral tradition. Heuristics are fine slider defaults; they are not findings. Retune everything.

  • Song, “Improving the Combat ‘Impact’ of Action Games” (2005) — frame freezing, directional camera shift, hit light, layered audio. Article ↗
  • Sakurai, Famitsu vol. 490–491 — hitstop scales with damage; both sides freeze equally. Translation ↗
  • SmashWiki, “Hitlag” — per-game formulas: Ultimate ≈15 frames at 15% damage, 30-frame cap. Wiki ↗
  • Lin et al. (2022) — Guilty Gear Xrd ≈7 light / ≈10 heavy frames; hit stop, sound consistency and camera control are the critical three. arXiv ↗
  • Jonasson & Purho, “Juice It or Lose It” (2012) — flash, squash, particles, the toggle-and-compare method. Demo ↗
  • Nijman, “The Art of Screenshake” (2013) — directional, fast-decaying shake; knockback as a layer.
  • Kao (2020) — moderate juiciness beats none and extreme: the overdone preset is the controlled failure case. DOI ↗
