How to generate a normal map

  1. Upload a sprite or texture — drag & drop or browse. Nothing is uploaded to any server.
  2. Tune: strength sets how tall the bumps look, smooth softens the height field, and the height source picks brightness (relief) or alpha (a bevel along the sprite's edges). Match the green channel to your engine — Y+ for Unity/Godot, Y− for DirectX.
  3. Preview & export: move your mouse over the lit preview to push the light around, then download the normal map PNG and assign it in your engine's 2D lighting.

Normal map FAQ

What is a normal map?

A normal map stores a surface direction per pixel in the RGB channels. A 2D engine or shader can then light a flat sprite as if it had bumps and bevels — the classic trick for lit pixel art characters, props and tiles.

How do I generate a normal map from a sprite?

Drop the PNG here, tune the strength and blur, then export. The height field comes from the sprite's brightness (or its alpha, which bevels the silhouette edges) and a Sobel filter turns it into per-pixel normals.

Do I need the green channel flipped for Unity or Godot?

It depends on the engine convention: OpenGL-style expects green pointing up, DirectX-style expects green pointing down. Use the green (Y) switch here to match your target — if a light looks inverted in your engine, just flip it.

Does the normal map work for pixel art sprites?

Yes. Keep smooth at 0–1 for crisp pixels and use the alpha height source so transparent edges get a bevel instead of a halo. The exported PNG keeps the original transparency, so the normal map layers 1:1 over the sprite.
